# Geog Ch7 Human-Environment Interaction

This chapter is about balance: how people depend on and change their environment, and what makes that sustainable.

## Must know
- Optimum population matches available resources, and over- and under-population are departures from it (9.6 §6.2–6.4).
- In Ethiopia, rapid growth has been linked to deforestation, pollution, species loss and land degradation (9.6 §6.5).
- Environmental determinism and possibilism are the two main theories on human-environment relations (10.6 §6.2).
- Sub-Saharan growth reflects late adoption and poor implementation of population policy (10.6 summary).
- Persistent changes include habitat loss, agricultural intensification, overexploitation and invasive species (11.6 §6.1).
- Poverty and environmental damage reinforce one another (11.6 §6.2).
- Environmental movements address problems through lobbying, education and activism (12.6 §6.4).
- Indigenous environmental knowledge is valuable but local, and has limits (12.6 §6.5).
