# Geog Ch2 Geology and Landforms

Ethiopia's rugged surface is a result of the same global processes that move the continents. Learn the local facts in Grade 9 and the theory in Grades 11 and 12.

## Must know
- Ethiopia lies within about 3°N–15°N and 33°E–48°E, and has a more or less compact shape (9.1 §1.2).
- It covers 1,106,000 square km and is the tenth largest country in Africa (9.1 §1.2).
- Key events: basement rocks in the Precambrian, peneplanation in the Paleozoic, sinking and uplift in the Mesozoic, and the Rift Valley in the Cenozoic (9.1 §1.3).
- Elevation ranges from Ras Dashen at 4620 m to the Kobar Sink at 116 m below sea level (9.1 §1.3).
- Africa's four landform regions: Alpine System, African Massif, East African Highlands and rift system, Southern Africa Platform (10.1 §1.3).
- Rocks are dated relatively or absolutely. Relative dating uses original horizontality, superposition and cross-cutting relationships (11.1 §1.2).
- Continental drift says today's continents came from Pangaea (12.1 §1.1).
- Plates move in divergent, convergent and transform ways, driven by convection currents among other forces (12.1 §1.2–1.3).
