# Geog Ch6 Economic Activities and Development

Why do some places develop faster? This chapter uses location, trade, technology and governance to answer. It overlaps strongly with Economics.

## Must know
- Economic activities are primary, secondary, tertiary, quaternary and quinary (9.5 §5.1).
- More than 80% of Ethiopians work in agriculture, which is mainly smallholder, subsistence and rain-dependent (9.5 §5.1–5.2).
- Overdependence on raw materials exposes African economies to income fluctuations and aid dependence (10.5 §5.2).
- Agenda 2063 is Africa's long-term plan. Learn how it compares with the SDGs (10.5 §5.4–5.5).
- Location theory includes agricultural location, industrial location and central place theories (11.5 §5.1).
- Climate extremes and poverty reinforce each other in a vicious cycle (11.5 §5.2).
- Landlocked countries pay more to trade. Ethiopia depends on Djibouti, Sudan and others (11.5 §5.3).
- Poverty has economic, cognitive, normative and power-politics elements (12.5 §5.1).
- Globalization has real benefits and real costs, and trade can widen inequality between regions (12.5 §5.2–5.3).
