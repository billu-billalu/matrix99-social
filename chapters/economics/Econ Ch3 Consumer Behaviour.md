# Econ Ch3 Consumer Behaviour

Consumers try to get the most satisfaction from a limited budget. Grade 10 measures utility with numbers, and Grade 11 ranks preferences using curves.

## Reading path
- [ ] [[Econ 10.1 Theory of Consumer Behaviour]]: study §1.1, 1.2, 1.3; skim §1.4
- [ ] [[Econ 11.1 Theory of Consumer Behavior and Demand]]: study §1.2, 1.3, 1.4; skim §1.1

## Must know
- Utility is the power of a good to satisfy wants. Cardinal utility is measured in utils, while ordinal utility only ranks (10.1 §1.1–1.2).
- Marginal utility falls as consumption increases (10.1 §1.2).
- Cardinal optimum for one good: MUx = Px. For several goods, MU ÷ price is equal across goods (10.1 §1.3).
- An indifference curve shows combinations giving equal satisfaction. Standard curves slope down, are convex, do not intersect, and higher means better (11.1 §1.2).
- The budget line slopes down with slope equal to the negative price ratio (11.1 §1.3).
- The consumer is in equilibrium at the tangency of the budget line and an indifference curve (11.1 §1.4).

## Formulas
- MU = change in TU ÷ change in quantity (10.1 §1.2)
- One good: MUx = Px. Two goods: MUx/Px = MUy/Py (10.1 §1.3)
- Budget line: PxX + PyY = M; slope = −Px/Py (11.1 §1.3)
