# Econ Ch5 Market Structures

The structure of a market decides how much power a firm has over price. Learn the structures in one comparison table.

## Reading path
- [ ] [[Econ 10.4 Market Structure]]: skim all
- [ ] [[Econ 11.2 Market Structure and the Decision of Firms]]: study §2.2, 2.3, 2.4, 2.5; skim §2.1

## Must know
- Criteria: number of buyers and sellers, product type, and freedom of entry and exit (10.4 summary).
- Perfect competition: many firms, homogeneous product, free entry, full information. The firm is a price taker, so P = AR = MR (10.4 §4.1).
- Monopoly arises from a key resource, a government right, or lower costs for one supplier (10.4 §4.2).
- Monopolistic competition has many firms, differentiated products and free entry (10.4 §4.3).
- Oligopoly has a few dominant firms and restricted entry (10.4 §4.4).
- Profit is highest where MR = MC. Compare price with AC at that output to see profit or loss (11.2 §2.2).
- A monopolist's price and quantity are linked, so MR falls as more units are sold (11.2 §2.3).

## Formulas
- TR = P × Q; AR = TR ÷ Q; MR = change in TR ÷ change in Q (10.4 §4.1)
- Profit = TR − TC, greatest where MR = MC (11.2 §2.2)
