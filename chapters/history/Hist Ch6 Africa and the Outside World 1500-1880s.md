# Hist Ch6 Africa and the Outside World 1500-1880s

This chapter explains how Africa's trade turned from the Sahara towards the Atlantic, and how the slave trade and early European settlement prepared the way for colonial rule.

## Must know
- Portugal made the first European contacts along West Africa, aiming to bypass Muslim North Africa and reach gold (9.6 §6.1).
- Three conditions helped the Trans-Atlantic trade start: broken-up large states, an existing slave-export role, and conflict among coastal states trading slaves for arms (9.6 §6.2).
- Millions of Africans were transported between the 15th and 19th centuries (11.6 §6.3).
- Industrialization helped end the slave trade in the early 19th century, leading to "legitimate trade" (11.6 §6.3–6.4).
- Domestic slavery and export to Arabia and the Ottoman Empire continued into the early 20th century (11.6 summary).
- In 1652 the Dutch set up a supply station in South Africa, which led to white settlement (9.6 §6.4).
- Missionaries and explorers often helped pave the way for colonialism (9.6 §6.5, 11.6 §6.6).

## Timeline
- 1652: Dutch settlement in South Africa
- Early 19th century: slave trade abolished; legitimate trade grows
