# Hist Ch16 Africa Since 1960

The form independence took depended largely on whether white settlers were present and whether the colonial power was willing to leave. Hopes then met conflict, debt and the "lost decade".

## Reading path
- [ ] [[Hist 10.7 Africa Since 1960]]: study §7.2; skim §7.1, 7.3

## Must know
- African nationalism grew after WWI, driven above all by Pan-Africanism (12.7 §7.1).
- The fifth Pan-African conference, held in Manchester in 1945, first demanded independence (12.7 §7.5, summary).
- Colonies without settlers gained independence more smoothly. Those with settlers, such as Algeria and Southern Rhodesia, fought armed struggles (10.7 §7.1, 12.7 §7.1).
- Portugal clung to its colonies, so they too had to fight (12.7 §7.1).
- The OAU helped liberate Africa but failed on peace and prosperity, and the AU replaced it (10.7 §7.1, 12.7 §7.5).
- Conflict, debt, drought and famine peaked in the 1980s, the "lost decade" (10.7 §7.3).

## Timeline
- 1919: first Pan-African conference referred to in Grade 12
- 1945: Manchester conference demands independence
- 1958: process of forming the OAU begins
- 1980s: "lost decade"
- Early 21st century: AU replaces OAU
