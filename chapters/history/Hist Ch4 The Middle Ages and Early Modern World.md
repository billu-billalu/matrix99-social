# Hist Ch4 The Middle Ages and Early Modern World

Trace how feudal Europe changed: trade and towns revived, new thinking spread, and exploration connected the world. Meanwhile, Islam, the Ottomans and China were major powers.

## Must know
- The West Roman Empire fell in 476 AD, while the Byzantine Empire continued for nearly another thousand years (11.4 summary).
- Justinian I (527–565 AD) took the Byzantine Empire to its greatest size (11.4 §4.1).
- In the manorial system, lords gave land to vassals for military service, and peasants worked it (11.4 §4.2).
- The Crusades followed Seljuk Turkish advances and helped revive trade (9.4 summary, 11.4 §4.2).
- Trade and towns revived from the 11th century, weakening the manor and helping capitalist relations develop (11.4 §4.2, §4.4).
- Islam rose in the 7th century and expanded under the Umayyad and Abbasid Caliphs, followed by the Ottomans (9.4 §4.2).
- China's history was understood through the dynastic cycle and the mandate of Heaven (9.4 §4.2).
- Martin Luther's reforms led to Protestantism, and the Discoveries sped up globalization (9.4 §4.4, §4.6).

## Timeline
- 476 AD: fall of the West Roman Empire
- 527–565 AD: reign of Justinian I
- 7th century: rise of Islam
- 11th century: revival of trade and towns; Crusades follow Seljuk advances
- 1453: Ottoman capture of Constantinople (see book check below)
