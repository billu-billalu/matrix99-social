# Hist Ch17 Ethiopia After 1991

After 1991 Ethiopia changed its state structure to ethnic federalism. Alongside this, the Nile became a central development and diplomatic question.

## Must know
- The Transitional Government ruled from 1991 to 1995, with an 87-member Council and a Charter creating 14 self-governing regions (10.8 §8.1).
- The 1995 Constitution established the FDRE, a federal and parliamentary system with the House of Peoples' Representatives and House of Federation (10.8 §8.1).
- The textbook counts nine regional states and two city councils. That figure reflects the book's date, so check the current number (10.8 §8.1).
- Ethiopia is the source of the Abay and Tekkeze and contributes about 86% of Nile waters (10.8 §8.2).
- The 1929 and 1959 agreements shared the Nile between Egypt and Sudan, and ignored upstream states (10.8 §8.2).
- Roads and education became top budget priorities (12.8 §8.3). The GERD was funded by Ethiopians and helped unite them for a common cause (12.8 §8.3).
- Ethiopia served in peacekeeping in Liberia, Rwanda, Sudan and Somalia (12.8 §8.4).

## Timeline
- 1929 and 1959: Nile water agreements
- 1991–1995: Transitional Government
- August 1995: Constitution takes effect
