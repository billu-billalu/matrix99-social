# Hist Ch13 The Age of World Wars

WWI's unresolved problems, the Russian Revolution and the Great Depression produced the dictators who led the world into WWII. Follow that chain of cause and effect.

## Reading path
- [ ] [[Hist 10.4 Society and Politics in the Age of World Wars]]: study §4.2, 4.3, 4.4, 4.5; skim §4.1, 4.6
- [ ] [[Hist 12.4 Society and Politics in the Age of World Wars]]: study §4.1, 4.3, 4.4; skim §4.2

## Must know
- The assassination of Franz Ferdinand was the trigger. Deeper causes were nationalism, imperial rivalry, an arms race, militarism and alliances (10.4 §4.1).
- WWI lasted from 1914 to 1918 between the Allied Powers and the Central Powers (10.4 §4.1, 12.4 §4.1).
- The Treaty of Versailles blamed and penalized Germany, and Hitler used that resentment (10.4 §4.5).
- In 1917, the February Revolution removed the Tsar and the October Revolution brought Lenin's Bolsheviks to power (12.4 §4.2).
- The League of Nations (1919) failed to stop aggression in the 1930s (10.4 §4.3).
- The Great Depression (1929–1933) helped militarists in Japan and dictators in Italy and Germany gain power (12.4 §4.3).
- WWII began on September 1, 1939 with the invasion of Poland, between the Axis and the Allies (12.4 §4.4).

## Timeline
- 1914–1918: World War I
- 1917: February and October revolutions in Russia
- 1919: League of Nations founded
- 1929–1933: Great Depression
- September 1, 1939: Germany invades Poland
- 1945: World War II ends
