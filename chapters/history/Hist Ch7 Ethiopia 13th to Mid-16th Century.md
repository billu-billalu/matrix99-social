# Hist Ch7 Ethiopia 13th to Mid-16th Century

Two forces dominate this chapter: competition for the Zeila trade, then the 16th-century upheaval of the Adal wars and the Oromo movement. Grade 9 is essential here, not optional.

## Must know
- The "Solomonic" dynasty replaced Zagwe in 1270, using the legend of Solomon and Sheba for legitimacy (9.7 §7.1, 11.7 §7.1).
- Wars among regional states largely concerned control of the Zeila trade, which also created economic interdependence (11.7 summary).
- Many states became tribute-paying provinces of the Christian kingdom until the late 15th century (11.7 §7.1).
- Southern and central states included Damot, Kafa, Ennarya, Bizamo, Walaita and the Agaw kingdom of Gojjam (9.7 §7.4).
- The Adal wars under Imam Ahmad caused great loss of life and destruction (11.7 §7.4).
- Oromo groups moved out from Bale from the early 1520s, changing the political map (11.7 §7.5).
- The Gadaa system was age-based and egalitarian (9.7 §7.6). Moggasa and Guddifacha are covered in 9.7 §7.7 only.

## Timeline
- 1270: "Solomonic" dynasty begins
- Late 15th century: end of the Christian kingdom's strongest tribute control
- 1520s–1559: Adal and Christian kingdom conflict
- Early 1520s: Oromo movements begin from Bale
